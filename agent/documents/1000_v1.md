# Strength Analysis Design Practice

## Introduction

### Purpose

The purpose of this document is to describe the activity to perform the static structural strength assessment of a Turbine Rear Structure (TRS) component. The analysis is done via a Finite Element Model (FEM).

### Background

The the design practice is the outcome of the preovious TRS-like product developments experiences and best practices known for safely certifying components.

### Definitions

From 25.301, 25.303 and 25.305.

- Limit Loads: the maximum loads to be expected in service. The structure must be able to support limit loads without detrimental permanent deformation.
- Ultimate Loads: Limit loads multiplied by prescribed factors of safety.
- Factor of safety: Unless otherwise specified, a factor of safety of 1.5 must be applied to the prescribed limit load which are considered external loads on the structure.

## Applicability

This document is applicable to a TRS due to the assumptions in this section.

This design practice is intended to comply with the airworthiness requirements for large commercial passanger aircraft as defined in both FAA's Title 14 of the CFR Part 25 Subpart C and EASA's CS 25.305 (The European and United States airworthiness bodies have a harmonized specification. Given the electronic accessibility of the CFR, it is taken as the master in this document). The design practice can also be used to comply with the Engine side of the requirements (14 CFR 33.23 and EASA CS-E), although the assumptions made here will have to be verified with the end customer.

This method is affected by other requirements in Part 25. The following list clarifies if what the design practice covers, not covers, assumes, and considers for the assessment of 25.305.

Subpart C

- 25.301 Loads - Considered
- 25.302 Interaction of systems and structures - Assumed worst time scenario of (factor of 1.5)
- 25.303 Factor of safety - Considered
- 25.305 Strength and deformation - Considered (a), (b). However (c) - (f) **not considered**  
- 25.307 Proof of structure - Considered
- 25.362 Engine failure loads - **Not considered**

Subpart D

- 25.613 Material strength properties and material design values.
- 25.619 Special factors - Considered.
- 25.621 Casting factors - Considered.
- 25.625 Fitting factors - Not considered - local effect.

Compliance to the requirements "not considered" or where assumptions have been identified could be modified, under approval from the Strength Subject Matter Expert (SME).

This design practice **does not cover** other requirements in Subpart C such as strength under dynamic loading, stability, fatigue or damage tolerance approaches (25.571). Any other requrements in Part 25 are not covered by this design practice.

## Method Description

### Inputs Required

Units: The analysis shall be conducted in [kg], [N], [m], [K] units always. If inputs are provided in other units, they shall be converted to these units for analysis. However, the documentation report may present different units (e.g. stresses in MPa, dimensions in mm).

#### Geometry

The nominal Geometry from the 3D CAD model shall be used for the analysis.

#### Finite Element Analysis Model

A mesh of the 3D CAD model shall be already available in order to calculate the TRS structural displancements. the internal load balance, the resultant stresses and strains throughout the structure, the component should be idealized with a complete 360 degrees elements, and connected via their interfaces with the neighbouring components: Nozzle, LPT case and Plug. Those components are considered "dummy parts" and shall also be meshed. Discrete element points shall be available to apply the mechanical interface loads to the model. In addition, the lug interfaces (port & starboard) are expected to be loaded, but no dummy needs to be attached to them. The entities where the boundary condition will be applied shall also be available in the model. The TRS shall be meshed with SOLID187 elements and with enough resolution to capture high stress areas. These entities in these areas shall be grouped for ease of post-processing.

#### Material

The following material data is required for the temperature range that the analysis will be calculated:

- Youngs Modulus (E) Average properties
- Yield stress (yield_stress, or YS) 99 percent probability with 95 percent confidence (25.613)
- Ultimate tensile stress (ultimate_stress, or UTS), (for ultimate analysis, 99 percent probability with 95 percent confidence (25.613))
- coeficient of thermal expansion Average properties

#### Loads

#### Mechanical Loads

Mechanincal loads consist of Force and Moment vectors applied at the interfaces, calculated from the whole engine model from the OEM.

They shall be applied at the interfaces between the TRS and neighbouring components. Except the bearing flange interface where the loads shall be ignored and boundary conditions applied instead.

If an ultimate analysis is required an no ultimate loads are provided by the OEM, an ultimate factor of 1.5 shall be applied to the limit loads during the initial loads processing to convert to ultimate loads.

Limit and ultimate loads shall be multiply by a factor of 1.25 (special factor). This special factor is in addition of the Ultimate Factor for the ultimate analysis.

#### Thermal loading

Thermal expansion shall be accounted for, as the TRS is under a high temperature environment. A worst operating Red Line temperature shall be provided (one constant, uniform value applicable to all loadcases). A 3D temperature map is not neccesary.

#### Pressure Loading

No pressure loading needs to be applied

### Overview of activities in the method

The activity process follows a standard Finite Element Analysis. The input preparation steps (1 & 2) may be skipped if the inputs are already provided in the appropriate format.

1. Prepare Loads
2. Prepare Material
3. Assemble FE Model
4. Solve FE Model
5. Post-process results
6. Write Documentation

##### Inputs:

## Criteria

### Limit loading analysis

#### Criteria L.1

For every loadcase evaluated, the maximum von Mises stress calculated by the FE model shall be lower than the yield strength of the material (average properties), at the actual temperature of the stress location. In terms of Margins of safety, the margin shall be positive. If this criteria is satisfied, no further criteria needs to be considered.

#### Criteria L.2

For the locations where the criteria 1 is not fulfilled, consider this criteria: The plastic von Mises strain shall be lower than 0.2% (0.002). The analysis can be carried out in the highest stressed node only, for each local region area exceeding limit stress.

#### Criteria L.3

For locations where criterias 1 and 2 are not fulfilled, consider this criteria: the permanent strain deformation shall be lower than 0.2% (0.002) under a non-linear FE solve.

### Ultimate loading analysis

#### Criteria U.1

For every loadcase evaluated, the maximum von Mises stress calculated by the FE model shall be lower than the ultimate strength of the material (average properties), at the actual temperature of the stress location. In terms of Margins of safety, the margin shall be positive. If this criteria is satisfied, no further criteria needs to be considered.

#### Criteria U.2

For the locations where the criteria U.1 is not fulfilled, consider this criteria: The plastic von Mises strain shall be lower than 0.2% (0.002). The analysis can be carried out in the highest stressed node only, for each local region area exceeding limit stress.

#### Criteria U.3

For locations where criterias U.1 and U.2 are not fulfilled, consider this criteria: the permanent strain deformation shall be lower than XXX under a non-linear FE solve.
